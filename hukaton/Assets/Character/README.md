# Reusable characters

The components in `Runtime` do not reference trading, UI panels, or a particular scene. They operate on a preconfigured player prefab. Configure references in the Inspector; no component assembles a player at runtime.

Drag `Prefabs/FirstPersonPlayer.prefab` into another scene, or create a prefab variant for a particular game layer. `Trading/Prefabs/Shopkeeper.prefab` is an example variant. Open `Scenes/MovementPlayground.unity` to try the player on its saved `MovementCourse` prefab: stairs, a slope, jump obstacles, and overhead clearance.

## Component responsibilities

- `PlayerControlGate` owns cursor capture and modal ownership. Escape releases the cursor and suppresses gameplay input. A click captures again; `CapturedThisFrame` prevents that click from also interacting with the world. Losing application focus releases capture. Each UI or game layer blocks using its own owner; one layer cannot release another layer's block.
- `FirstPersonInput` samples movement, mouse look, sprint and jump. Input is cleared while capture is unavailable.
- `FirstPersonMotor` moves the authored `CharacterController`: acceleration, braking, air control, jump buffering, coyote time, gravity and stamina use. `Teleport(position, rotation)` clears motion and aligns yaw and view pitch. Velocity is what input and pushes ask for: a wall (anything steeper than the slope limit) only removes the part of it that drove into the wall and did not get through, so the player slides along walls and climbs steps at full speed. The capsule's displacement is never fed back as velocity, so the controller pushing the capsule out of a corner or a narrow gap cannot bounce it from side to side.
  - **Ground snapping.** Downhill the ground falls away faster than one frame of gravity pulls the body (walking 4 m/s on slopes over ~30°, sprinting on slopes over ~20°, every step's edge), and a bare `CharacterController` hops from frame to frame. After the move, a body that stood last frame, was not launched (jump, mushroom bounce, upward impulse, updraft) and has no ground contact is swept down (capsule cast, triggers ignored, layers from the physics matrix for the player's layer plus the controller's include/exclude layers) and put back onto walkable ground within reach: the step offset, or the drop of the steepest walkable slope over the distance just moved, whichever is larger, plus the skin, at most `maxGroundSnap` (0.5 m). A step's edge, which the rounded capsule bottom touches with a slanted normal, counts when the face just behind the edge is walkable. The snap never collects walls and the running speed is taken from the move before it. A real ledge (0.8 m) is out of reach and is still a fall.
  - **Landing only after a real fall.** `Landed` (landing dip, landing sound, fall damage, heavy-landing dust, mushroom bounce) fires only when the body dropped at least `minLandingDrop` (0.3 m) below the highest point since it left the ground, or was in the air for at least `minLandingAirTime` (0.2 s), and hits faster than 2.5 m/s. Jumps (1.2 m outdoors, 0.45 m in the shop) land once as before. A body sent downward in the air (rope release, push) counts the height a fall from rest needs for its speed, so a hard release still hurts.
  - **Feedback grace.** `IsGrounded` stays the physical contact (mushroom caps, updraft, vitals, tree ally). Presentation reads `GroundedForFeedback`: grounded, or only just off the ground (`feedbackGraceTime`, 0.1 s) without having been launched; `AirTime` gives the seconds in the air. Head bob, footsteps and the card hand bob use it, and sprinting survives coyote time, so the sprint FOV does not flicker over bumps. `GroundNormal` is the face under the capsule's axis (a step's flat tread, a ramp's surface).
- `PlayerStamina` owns the stamina budget. Its independent `StaminaState` implements delay, recovery and exhaustion hysteresis. `PlayerStamina.Upgrade` is the PC's «Выносливость» (`StaminaUpgrade`, pure): every bought step adds `upgradeSecondsPerStep` (1 s) of sprint, prices in `upgradePrices` (200 / 450 / 800 / 1250 / 1800: 5 » 10 s). The serialized `capacity` stays the base; a step (or a loaded save) resizes the budget and fills it (`StaminaState.Resize`). The game save keeps the level (`GameSave.player.staminaLevel`).
- `StaminaPips` (UI, `Game/Prefabs/UI/Stamina pips.prefab`) draws stamina as an arc of pips on a circle around the crosshair (radius 21 = the root's offset under the aim, 8 apart along the arc, at most 180°, so ten pips reach the sides of the aim), one instance inside each HUD's 736 × 368 layout (shop and outing), fed every frame by its HUD (`Present`). One pip = one step = 1 s of sprint (`StaminaGauge`: lit while any stamina is left in it). It shows only while stamina is not full (sprinting, recovering, exhausted) and fades out 0.6 s after it is full again. A spent pip pops up and goes hollow, refilled pips hop, and out of breath every pip is hollow and red until the recovery threshold. All motion is in whole layout units; nothing rotates or scales. Art: `Game/Art/Ui/ui_stamina_pip_{full,empty,tired}.png` (6 × 6, Point, uncompressed).
- `FirstPersonView` controls yaw/pitch and modest movement feedback. The camera's authored position, rotation and FOV are the baseline. `motionIntensity = 0` disables bob, roll and landing dip; `sprintFov = 0` disables the sprint FOV change.
  - **Step smoothing.** A step lifts or drops the body by its height within a frame or two. While the body is on foot in both frames (and the change is at most `maxStepSmoothing`, 0.45 m, over less than 1 m), the eye keeps its height through the part of the change that goes beyond the slope under the feet (`GroundNormal`, capped at `stepSlopeAllowance` = 35°, plus a little slack) and eases back with `stepSmoothTime` (0.08 s, critically damped, offset at most 0.45 m). Ramps up to 35° are followed exactly; going down stairs the eye glides at the stairs' average incline instead of dropping step by step, and a step up is never popped into the eye in one frame. Jumps, falls and teleports are shown as they are; `ResetPose` and `TeleportKeepingVelocity` clear the offset. It is applied as a world-up offset in the camera parent's space and does not depend on `motionIntensity`; `stepSmoothTime = 0` disables it.
- Optional `FirstPersonAudio` produces footsteps by actual travelled distance and listens to jump/landing events. Its clips and AudioSource are authored assets/references.

## Authored hierarchy

Player root: `CharacterController`, control gate, input, motor, stamina, view and optional audio. A `PitchPivot` child sits at eye height; its camera child starts at local position zero. Assign the root to `yawRoot`, pivot to `pitchPivot`, and camera transform to `cameraTransform`. Objects held by the character can be children of the camera; they are outside this module's responsibilities.

Use a dedicated AudioSource for movement so other game sounds do not share step pitch variation. Layer-specific prefabs can be variants of the base player prefab.

## Temporarily owning control

```csharp
// Opening a conversation or another modal interaction.
control.SetBlocked(this, true);
motor.Teleport(conversationPose.position, conversationPose.rotation);

// Closing it restores capture only when it was captured before the first modal owner.
control.SetBlocked(this, false);
```

Always release your own claim on disable/destruction. World interaction should require `control.GameplayActive && !control.CapturedThisFrame`. `PlayerStamina.Normalized` (or `Value` / `Capacity` / `StaminaPerPip`) supplies a HUD gauge without a dependency from the player to any UI.

`FirstPersonModalView` provides a fixed camera without moving the character body. Assign the camera, shared control gate and an authored full-screen black `CanvasGroup`. `Enter(pose, fov)` and `SetPose(pose, fov)` switch to external scene markers under a 0.2 s blink; `Exit()` restores the FPS camera and releases only this component's control claim. Disable also restores the camera and clears its claim. It is independent of the trading layer; `TradingShopView` uses it for the counter and display viewpoints. Transition timings and FOV are editable in the Inspector.

## Tuning

Movement defaults: walk 4 m/s, sprint 6.5 m/s, acceleration 18 m/s², braking 24 m/s², jump height 1.2 m. Jump buffer 0.14 s and coyote time 0.12 s permit slightly early/late inputs. Sprint uses 20 stamina/s, jump costs 12; recovery starts after 1 s at 25/s. Exhaustion requires recovery to 25% before sprinting is available again.

Ground snap reach at most 0.5 m; a landing needs a 0.3 m drop or 0.2 s in the air; feedback grace 0.1 s; step smoothing 0.08 s up to 0.45 m, slopes up to 35° followed exactly.

Base camera FOV is 75°. Walk bob amplitude is 5.5 cm with a slower sideways sway and 0.65° gait roll; sprint increases the amplitude by 1.45× and the pace from 1.8 to 2.5 Hz. Motion blends in and out with actual movement. Strafe roll is 0.8°, landing dip at most 4.5 cm, sprint FOV +5°. These values are Inspector fields and can be reduced independently.

EditMode tests are in `Hakaton.Character.Tests` and cover the stamina budget, its resize, the «Выносливость» steps, the pip arc (count, lit, exhausted, whole-unit layout) and independent control owners.

PlayMode tests are in `Hakaton.Character.PlayModeTests`. They instantiate the saved player prefab on a disposable physics surface and drive keyboard input through Unity's Input System to check walking, sprinting, braking, jumping, landing, stamina consumption, modal control ownership, sliding along a wall and standing still (no side-to-side bouncing) when pushed into a narrow wedge, staying grounded with a steady eye while walking and sprinting down 30° and 40° ramps, walking down six 0.25 m steps (no landing, the eye never rises and glides without sudden speed changes, then settles at standing height), walking up steps without an eye pop, still falling and landing once from a 0.8 m ledge at 30 fps, and a jump on a ramp that is not pulled back onto it. The slope and stair tests fix the frame time with `Time.captureDeltaTime` (reset in teardown) and sample the camera after the view's LateUpdate. Run them in the Unity Test Runner's PlayMode tab in the Editor; the fixture loads its prefab through AssetDatabase.

## Sprite NPCs

`Prefabs/SpriteNpc.prefab` is a reusable, authored visitor rig. Its `SpriteNpcActor` has no dependency on trading. Assign an external entrance-to-destination `route` and the viewing camera's transform to `viewer` on the scene instance. The actor root sits at the feet; the `Facing` child provides yaw billboarding and the turn; `Foot anchored step` provides distance-driven Y scaling. The SpriteRenderer uses the shared `HandDrawnSprite` material, with a per-renderer UV rectangle for the current atlas cell. Edit mode previews the authored sprite without starting a visit.

NPCs, monsters and world loot share **Pixels Per World Unit** (`_PixelsPerUnit`) in `Assets/Rendering/Materials/HandDrawnSprite.mat`. Higher values give finer pixels; lower values give larger blocks. The shared density is **500 pixels per world unit**, giving the 25 cm counter goblin about 125 blocks across its visible height. This replaces the initial bee-swarm calibration of 184.875 so small characters retain readable facial details. Spawn-size multipliers do not change this constant: differently sized creatures keep the same world pixel size. The shader measures the rendered sprite plane, including parent scale, flips and the active atlas cell. Its grid moves with the drawing and naturally becomes smaller on screen with distance. There are no per-creature/loot pixel-size fields or overrides to maintain. Each world pixel is one hard point sample of the source (no filtering). Sprites take the scene fog like the rest of the world. The bubble and suspension-rope material variants inherit the same density while preserving their animation settings. `WorldSpritePixelTests` covers the GPU grid and the shared material setup. Camera-relative coordinates come from the inverse view matrix, so an overridden camera view works even when its Transform position differs. The regression tests cover equivalent PPU/Transform combinations and the tutorial bush at its cave coordinates.

`NpcAppearance` assets hold the name, body scale, neutral/front, back, displeased, happy and angry sprites. The saved scale of `stepRoot` represents the default appearance; other appearance scales are relative to it. All views must share the canvas size, pivot and PPU. The existing peasant and blacksmith appearances and prefab variants are in `Assets/Content/NPCs/`.

Call `BeginVisit(appearance)` while idle; wait for `ReadyForTrade` before starting a conversation. Use `SetExpression` for in-conversation feedback and `Leave(outcome)` to show the result, turn with a midpoint swap to the back sprite, and retrace the route. `Cancel()` skips the reaction hold but still turns and walks out. `IsBusy` remains true until arrival at the exit, so a subsequent visit cannot teleport the current one. Cancellation during arrival returns through the waypoints already reached.

Movement and departure timings are serialized Inspector fields. `SpriteNpcActorTests` covers turn/sprite timing, interruption on a corner, distance-driven gait, feet alignment and frame-independent travel. Trading's PlayMode fixture checks the integration against its saved scene.
